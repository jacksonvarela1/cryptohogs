The officers

The people behind the coin.

Ten students and one professor with a shared conviction: Arkansas shouldn't watch the future of money from the bleachers. Here's who they are and what they're building.

President

Lucas Vilar.

Lucas runs the show. Born in Brazil and fluent in Portuguese, English, and Spanish, he came up through Nashville, Arkansas, where he was a robotics team president, a regional chess champion, and a varsity goalkeeper before carrying a 4.0 into the Walton College as a business student and a statewide Academic All-Star.

At Arkansas he led Crypto Hogs from a group chat to one of the fastest-moving new organizations on campus, co-hosted Fayetteville's slice of PizzaDAO's global Bitcoin Pizza Day alongside Stand With Crypto Arkansas, and spends his weeks putting founders, executives, and policy leaders in front of the club. The stated ambition is global entrepreneur. The club is the proof of concept.

Vice President

Reese Crawford.

A co-founder of the club and its industry engine. Reese studies Information Systems with an Emerging Technologies concentration at the Walton College, carries a 4.0 and a Chancellor's List nod, and graduates December 2026.

He's already deep in the industry: research fellow at GOSH AI and SOLV Protocol, business development and research at Red Beard Ventures, and the person behind the club's first protocol partnerships, including a Sui Foundation grant.

Treasurer

Paxton Crocker.

Paxton keeps the books and reads the world. A Little Rock native and Walton College student, he founded Black Barrel Intel, an independent research platform covering energy markets, commodity flows, and geopolitical risk, with stops at Startup Junkie and the McMillon Innovation Studio's Sam's Club team along the way.

The club's money answers to him, and so do the spreadsheets.

Director of Strategy

Brixton Blasi.

Finance major at the Walton College and co-founder and Head of Growth at Swaply, the student-built campus marketplace at Arkansas, where he runs the ambassador and creator programs that took the app past 200 verified students. A former Texas 6A varsity goalkeeper, he's on record calling startup building "more valuable than any class," which is exactly the energy a strategy seat needs.

Where the club goes next is his favorite question.

Faculty Advisor

Dr. Daniel Conway.

When the United States decides what blockchain standards should be, Dr. Conway is in the chair. He leads INCITS/Blockchain, the U.S. technical committee that represents the country to ISO's global blockchain standards body, co-chairs technical standards for the Global Blockchain Business Council's Global Standards Mapping Initiative, and serves as blockchain standards chief at KoreInside.

At Arkansas he is Teaching Professor of Information Systems at the Walton College and Associate Director of its Blockchain Center of Excellence. The resume before that: a Ph.D. in Operations Research from Indiana, faculty posts at Notre Dame, Indiana, Northwestern, Iowa, Florida, and Virginia Tech, Cisco Systems' first professor in residence, and consulting for Cisco, John Deere, and AIG. His research runs through MIS Quarterly Executive and IEEE Engineering Management Review, and his crypto policy op-eds run regularly in RealClearMarkets.

"Blockchains do not eliminate trust, they reallocate it." That's his line, and this club is where he hands it down. The most unfair academic advantage in the SEC.
